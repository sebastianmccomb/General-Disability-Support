Seven senses 0 words

Sight and hearing fill up on their own. Organic (heartbeat, breath, muscle tension) and kinesthetic (your body's relation to the space around it) are the two everyone forgets — and the two that make writing breathe.

Diving into

His own exercises never start blank — "pick an object at random," "dive into homelessness for ten minutes." One roll at a time on purpose, not a list to pick your favourite from — the point is diving into something you wouldn't have chosen.

Object writing

Now focus it. A lyric starts from one of three places — an emotion, a calculated idea, or a title — and then the work is finding the angle. Interrogating your own title is what turns a phrase into a song with somewhere to go.

Vowel spread

Six rhyme types, ordered most stable to least — like chord voicings from root position outward. Reach down the list when you want a line to stay unresolved. Dashed words are on Pattison's cliché list. Click to keep; kept words collect in the palette below.

Boxes are ideas, not lines — Pattison calls them form-neutral: they show how the idea evolves, whatever song form you end up in. Each one should be bigger than the last. If all three are the same size you have a travelogue: verses that share a title but never build, like three separate avalanches a third of the way up the mountain. Write the idea first; the lines come after.

Travelogue test

Drop the chorus and read the verses back to back. If you can't tell what's happening without the title holding them together, the boxes aren't gaining weight.

His moves ch. 24

  • Work the trigger line. Last line before the chorus — it colours everything the chorus says. Check it early.
  • Ground the metaphor. Sea spray on his face fails if they're indoors. It has to have a real place in the scene.
  • Pick the rhyme type for the feeling. Want the section closed? Perfect. Want it left hanging? Reach down the scale.
  • Get people off stage. He said goodbye three lines ago — don't let him linger.
  • Suggest, don't state. "Sand and shifting dunes" carries the sands of time without saying it.
  • Check every pronoun. If a listener can't tell who she is, the verse fails.
